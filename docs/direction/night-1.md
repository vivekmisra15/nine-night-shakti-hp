# Night 1 direction: Shailaputri, daughter of the mountain

Status: approved 16 October 2026; revised 17 October 2026 after the first build. **The revision below takes priority over everything after it.** Where a beat's notes further down disagree with it (for example drawn bodies for Shiva or Sati, or captions), follow the revision.

## Revision, 17 October 2026

### Look

- **Iconographic and simple:** night skies, mountain lines, soft glowing light, fine gold line-art. Keep the colour identity, palettes, signature flame, timing to the narration, freeze-frames, slow motion, gentle camera moves, layered depth and atmosphere below. Leave out anything that adds detail without meaning.
- **Formless divine figures:** Shiva is a cool silver light. Sati and Shailaputri are a warm flame (the signature flame). Added 18 October 2026: at Shailaputri's settling in Beat 8, a very faint fine-line head-and-shoulders outline shows within her light, as on the other nights; never a body or features. Vishnu is a soft blue light, and Virabhadra a towering column of light.
- **People as fine gold outlines:** Daksha, Sati's mother and sisters, Shiva's attendants, Narada, Himavat and Mena, the gods and the assembly. Simple faces or none, and never filled costume figures.
- **Harry Potter scenes:** abstract as before, with letterbox bars.
- **Captions** describe sounds only. Never put invented lines of text over the picture.
- **Ambience, not hiss:** long stretches of filtered noise sound like white noise, so there are no continuous wind, room or crowd beds. Wind is a soft gust now and then; only the fire crackle, Beat 8's stream and the opening of Beat 6's storm are continuous, and quiet.

### Changes to particular beats

- **Beat 2:** Shiva and Sati by the fire become two lights by a small fire: silver and warm. The campfire is smaller and redder than Sati's flame, so the two are never confused. Daksha is an outline on his throne.
- **Beat 3:** Sati at the cliff edge is her flame, leaning towards the passing trails of light.
- **Beat 4:** Shiva's warning is his light brightening gently. At the reveal, Sati's flame travels down the valley path.
- **Beat 5:** the mother and sisters, the attendants and Daksha are outlines. Sati is her flame from the start, and the guardrail is unchanged: the flame rises and goes out, with no body and no fire around a figure. The parent note is a notification (the bell), not a screen that stops the story.
- **Beat 6:** Shiva's light carries her dimmed flame across the land. As Vishnu's soft light rises, it scatters into petals of light that become the Shakti Peethas. No body is drawn.
- **Beat 7:** Daksha, restored, kneels as an outline with a quiet outline goat's head, before Shiva's silver light.
- **Beat 8:** the flame relights on the peak and settles as Shailaputri's light. Her five symbols (bull, trident, lotus, crescent moon, mountain) are small gold outline icons around it, and they are the tappable hotspots. Himavat and Mena are kneeling outlines.
- **Beat 9 (the theme, Rootedness):** a single diya on the mountain, with fine roots of golden light growing slowly down into the rock. Shown faintly behind the theme's words.

### Screens

- **Layout:** a 16:9 picture on the left, text on the right (on a phone, the picture above the text), and no page scrolling.
- **Text size:** at least 18 px, and about 100 words at most per step.
- **The text pane:** never more than about 40% of the width.
- **Text-led steps** (ideas, comparison, reflection) are full width over a faint backdrop of night sky and flame.

### Narration display and pace

- **Stories and retellings** appear sentence by sentence. The current sentence is bright and the previous one or two are dimmed.
- **Earlier sentences slide up** over about 0.6 s, only when a new sentence starts. There is no continuous scroll.
- **Tapping a dimmed sentence**, or dragging the progress bar, moves the text and the animation together.
- **Reading pace:** Relaxed (about 130 words a minute), Slower (about 40% longer; the default) or Tap to continue.
- **The Harry Potter retellings** are narrated this way too, and each scene's animation is timed to its retelling.

### Splash and map

- **Splash (revised 18 October 2026):** one screen over a night sky with slow stars and a soft glow: the app's name small at the top, the large question *What is power for?*, the four questions (from the Recurring themes table in `docs/NINE_NIGHTS_CONTENT.md`) fading in one at a time, the introduction and the note ("The opening splash" in the same file), then "Begin the journey", the music switch and volume. Begin starts the music softly and goes to the map. The one-sentence disclaimer and a link to the full Disclaimer page are at the bottom. The spoiler notice and "How we compare" follow, once, before Night 1.
- **Map:** a constellation of nine diyas on a winding dotted path (top to bottom on a phone). Completed nights glow, Night 1 is highlighted, and unbuilt nights are faded and read "being written". Night 1 shows शैलपुत्री beside Shailaputri.

## Night 1 identity

**Colour identity: "snow, flame and dawn".** Moonlit silver-white snow and deep indigo night belong to Kailasa. Saffron and white-gold belong to the lamp flame. The night ends in rose-gold Himalayan dawn. Daksha's court is the night's single heavy colour, burnished gold and temple red, so it feels like the wrong house for Sati. The Hogwarts scenes use their own cooler, desaturated candle-amber and ink-blue palette, so the two worlds never blur. The Navaratri "colour of the day" (a 2003 newspaper campaign) is not used.

**Signature motif: one diya flame.** The night is told through a single lamp:
- It lights at the opening.
- It is Sati's flame in Beat 5, rising and going out.
- It is relit on the mountain peak at her rebirth in Beat 8.
- At the close it becomes the first lit diya on the nine-night map.

A second recurring shape is the mountain ridge line. It is the horizon of every traditional beat, and it becomes Shailaputri's silhouette in Beat 8.

**Musical motif.** The bansuri plays a rising phrase in Raga Bhupali: *Sa Re Ga Pa* (D E F♯ A), "the mountain rising". The answer falls: *Dha Pa Ga Re Sa* (B A F♯ E D), "coming home". The motif is heard whole only at the opening, at the rebirth (Beat 8) and at the close. Elsewhere only fragments return.

**Music settings**
- **Root:** D, with the tanpura on Sa–Pa (D3, A3) for all traditional beats.
- **Tempo:** 56–72 BPM for most of the night, peaking at about 96 for Virabhadra (Beat 6).
- **Signature instrument:** bansuri.

**Raga choices** (comment each one in the score code)

| Raga | Notes on D | Mood | Used in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bhupali | S R G P D (pentatonic; no Ma or Ni) | Serene devotion, open and bright, like the mountain | Opening, Beat 2, Beat 8, closing |
| Bageshri | S R g m P D n (komal Ga and Ni) | Longing; Sati missing her mother and sisters | Beats 3–4 |
| Marwa | S r G M D N (komal Re, tivra Ma, no Pa) | Twilight unease that never settles, because there is no Pa to rest on | Beats 5–7 |
| Yaman | S R G M P D N (tivra Ma) | Tender evening, warm and reflective | Beats 9, 15 and 16 (before the close) |

**Hogwarts palette (Beats 10–13), kept separate**
- **Instruments:** celesta-like bells, harp, soft strings and low woodwind (clarinet or bassoon-like voices).
- **Mode:** D Dorian, in 4/4 or free time.
- **Guardrail:** no melody in 3/8 waltz time, no minor-key celesta melody that leaps around, and nothing resembling any film theme. Motifs move stepwise within a narrow range.

**Adaptive layers, used throughout**
1. **Drone:** tanpura, or a low string pad for Hogwarts.
2. **Motif:** bansuri or santoor, or celesta and harp for Hogwarts.
3. **Percussion:** tabla, mridangam and low drums.
4. **Strings:** sarangi-like voice and soft strings.
5. **Climax:** low percussion, swelling strings and conch.
6. **Resolution:** temple bell, singing bowl and a warm pad.

Each beat's intensity (1–10) sets which layers are active. All audio runs through a master limiter.

**Opening invocation (7 s), at the start of Beat 1 after the spoiler and "how we compare" screens**
- **0–1.5 s:** black. A soft temple bell; the tanpura fades in.
- **1.5–3 s:** a single diya ignites at centre frame. The flame sways and its warm reflection lies on stone.
- **3–5.5 s:** the camera pulls back and up. The diya sits at the foot of a snow mountain under stars. A faint nine-petal mandala turns once behind the peak. The bansuri plays *Sa Re Ga Pa*.
- **5.5–7 s:** the title fades in. The tanpura holds.

The "Continue" tap on the spoiler screen is the user gesture that allows audio to start.

**Closing (8 s), after the Beat 16 takeaway**
- The mountain moves from deep night to the first rose light of dawn.
- The diya flame shrinks to a calm ember and rises out of frame as a single spark.
- Cross-fade to the nine-night map, where diya 1 lights.
- **Music:** the bansuri plays the falling answer *Dha Pa Ga Re Sa*, then one temple bell, then a 5 s tanpura tail.

## Shared conventions

- **Freeze-frames:** dim the scene by 20%, stop particles, and let the music fall back to the drone.
- **Slow-motion windows:** play at 0.35× speed and drop the music to half tempo.
- **Choice beats:** while the choice cards are open, hold the last frame of the story behind them with gentle breathing motion.
- **Activities:** the score ducks to the drone only, at −12 dB, and the timer gives a soft wooden tick once each 10 s.

## Beat 1: Before we begin (opening, contemporary)

1. **Script:** night-1.md, Beat 1 (spoiler notice, "how we compare", what Navaratri is), then the opening invocation.
2. **Environment:** night, clear and still.
   - Foreground: stone ledge with the diya.
   - Midground: a ring of nine unlit diyas, faint.
   - Background: snow ridge and a starfield.
3. **Palette:** `kailasa`.
4. **Lighting:** the diya is the key light; cool moonlight is the rim light; a soft star glow fills the ambient. The mood is hushed and expectant.
5. **Camera:** after the invocation, a slow push-in on the diya over 30 s while the text panels show (wide shot, eye level, gentle lens feel).
6. **Characters:** none.
7. **VFX:**
   - Flame flicker reacts to the music (±3% scale).
   - Faint golden dust drifts upward (opacity 0.15).
   - The mandala makes one rotation per 60 s at 8% opacity.
   - As each regional tradition is named, one of the nine diyas glows briefly.
8. **Transition:** the diya flame brightens and then splits into two warm points, which become Kailasa's fire and Daksha's palace in Beat 2.
9. **Intensity: 2.**

**Music:**
- **Instruments:** tanpura enters; bansuri motif (whole); temple bell at the start.
- **Tempo and dynamics:** 60 BPM, pp to p, very sparse.
- **Register:** meditative.
- **Cues:** bell at frame 0, motif when the mountain is revealed.
- **Ambience:** a faint night wind.

## Beat 2: The daughter who chose (story, tradition)

1. **Script:** Beat 2: Daksha's pride; Sati loves Shiva; the marriage only because Brahma asks; Kailasa.
2. **Environment:** two places, then one.
   - Opening: Daksha's palace by day. Foreground: carved pillars and ritual vessels. Midground: rows of courtiers. Background: gold domes.
   - Cross-fade to Kailasa at dusk. Foreground: snowy rock and a small fire. Midground: Shiva and Sati seated. Background: the peak and the first stars.
3. **Palette:** `dakshaCourt` changing to `kailasa`.
4. **Lighting:**
   - Palace: hard, high golden key light; cold and formal.
   - Kailasa: firelight key, moon rim, a warm and intimate mood.
5. **Camera:**
   - Palace: a slow low-angle tilt up to Daksha enthroned (12 s).
   - Kailasa: a sweeping aerial glide up the mountain (15 s).
   - End: a gentle push-in to a medium two-shot of Shiva and Sati (15 s).
6. **Characters:**
   - Daksha: upright, chin raised, still. Proud, not monstrous.
   - Shiva: ash-blue skin, matted hair with a crescent moon, a stylised serpent; eyes half-closed and calm.
   - Sati: red sari, graceful posture, a soft smile; she turns towards Shiva.
   - Wild spirits: drifting silhouettes of light, playful rather than scary.
   - Movement is slow and fluid. Emotional intensity is low.
7. **VFX:**
   - Palace: incense smoke curls rightward (opacity 0.3).
   - Kailasa: fire embers rise slowly; drifting snow; fireflies around the spirit silhouettes.
   - Freeze-frame on the two-shot as the caption reaches "lived happily on Mount Kailasa".
8. **Transition:** snow turns to falling marigold petals, which lead into the palace preparations of Beat 3.
9. **Intensity: 3.**

**Music:**
- **Raga:** Bhupali.
- **Palace section:** tanpura, plus a stiff, formal harmonium-like drone at 66 BPM.
- **Kailasa section:** santoor enters, then a light tabla dadra at 66–70 BPM.
- **Dynamics and density:** p, light.
- **Register:** serene to hopeful.
- **Cues:** the harmonium drops out at the cross-fade to Kailasa; a bansuri fragment (*Sa Re Ga*) at the two-shot.
- **Ambience:** palace hum at first; mountain wind and fire crackle on Kailasa.

## Beat 3: A sacrifice without Shiva (story, tradition)

1. **Script:** Beat 3: the gathering where all rise except Brahma and Shiva; Daksha's curse; a grand sacrifice without Shiva; the gods' wives pass overhead; Sati's longing.
2. **Environment:**
   - Assembly hall. Foreground: a row of standing figures in silhouette. Midground: two seated figures. Background: Daksha.
   - Then Kailasa at twilight. Foreground: Sati at the cliff edge. Midground: clouds. Background: the sky crossed by trails of light (the gods' wives on their way).
3. **Palette:** `dakshaCourt` changing to `kailasa`, with saffron light trails.
4. **Lighting:**
   - Hall: a red-gold key light; Daksha's shadow grows long as he speaks.
   - Cliff: twilight blue ambient, with a warm glow from the passing trails; the mood is wistful.
5. **Camera:**
   - Hall: a wide frontal shot, then a slow push-in on Daksha as he curses (10 s).
   - Cliff: an over-the-shoulder shot from behind Sati, then a slow orbit to her face (20 s).
6. **Characters:**
   - Daksha's arm sweeps out, firmly but not violently.
   - Sati's hand rises to her heart and her gaze follows the trails. Her longing shows in a slight lean forward.
   - Emotional intensity is medium.
7. **VFX:**
   - About 8 shimmering trails cross from left to right in a gentle arc, with gold sparkles (opacity 0.6).
   - A slow-motion window as Sati watches the trails, 6 s.
   - Freeze-frame on Sati's face.
8. **Transition:** the last trail curves down into the valley and becomes a glowing path, which is the path of Beat 4.
9. **Intensity: 4.**

**Music:**
- **Raga:** Bageshri.
- **Instruments:** tanpura; a sarangi-like voice enters at the curse; the bansuri carries the melody at the cliff.

- **Dynamics:** mp, with sparse density.
- **Register:** tense, then sorrowful.
- **Cues:** a low drum stroke when Daksha speaks the curse; a shimmer of small temple chimes as the trails pass.
- **Ambience:** distant festive bells from the valley.

## Beat 5: At her father's house (story, tradition)

The "For the parent" note shows before this beat; it is the app's content screen, not part of the animation.

1. **Script:** Beat 5: the welcome; Daksha's silence; no share for Shiva; Sati stops the attendants; her speech; her meditation facing north; she leaves her body (BP 4.4).
2. **Environment:** the sacrificial ground at dusk. Foreground: the altar fire pit and offering vessels. Midground: the assembly in silhouette. Background: the palace and darkening sky.
3. **Palette:** `dakshaCourt`, desaturating towards `grief` after the flame goes out.
4. **Lighting:**
   - Welcome: warm lamp light around the mother and sisters.
   - Daksha: cold top light.
   - Her speech: a gold rim light on Sati.
   - The flame moment: the only light is the lamp, and every other light dims to 10%.
   - The mood moves from warm to solemn.
5. **Camera:**
   - A medium shot of the embrace (8 s).
   - A slow pan to the empty seat where Shiva's share should be (6 s).
   - A low-angle shot of Sati as she speaks (12 s).
   - Then a slow devotional push-in, not on Sati but on a single diya placed before her (20 s).
6. **Characters:**
   - The mother and sisters run with outstretched arms.
   - Daksha turns his head away.
   - Sati lifts her palm and the attendants' silhouettes stop. She stands tall as she speaks, with steady gestures.
   - She sits and closes her eyes, then fades softly out of the frame before the flame rises. Her body is never shown at the moment she leaves it.
7. **VFX (guardrail):**
   - A single lamp flame grows upward in a slow-motion window (8 s), brightens to white-gold, then thins and goes out.
   - A thin smoke thread rises (opacity 0.4) and fades.
   - There is no fire around any figure.
   - Freeze-frame on the empty lamp.
   - Then "a great cry across the worlds": a soft radial wave of light passes outward, and every assembly silhouette turns towards Daksha.
8. **Transition:** the smoke thread becomes the dark storm cloud of Beat 6.
9. **Intensity: 7** (emotional rather than kinetic).

**Music:**
- **Raga:** Marwa.
- **Instruments:**
  - Welcome: santoor, softly.
  - Daksha's silence: the santoor stops.
  - Her speech: sarangi-like strings rise, with a slow mridangam heartbeat at 64 BPM.
  - The flame: everything except the tanpura falls away. The bansuri holds one long note that fades with the flame, and then there are 3 s of near-silence (tanpura at −24 dB).
  - The cry: a low strings swell with wind; no voices.
- **Dynamics:** p, rising to mf, then ppp.
- **Register:** tense, then sorrowful, then transcendent.
- **Cues:** the drum stops when Sati raises her palm; the bansuri note is tied to the flame.

## Beat 6: Shiva's grief (story, tradition, combined telling)

1. **Script:** Beat 6: Narada brings the news; the lock of hair and Virabhadra (BP 4.5). In later texts: wandering, Vishnu, and the Shakti Peethas (Kamakhya, Kalighat). Shiva's silent meditation.
2. **Environment:**
   - Part one: storm over Kailasa. Foreground: rock. Midground: Shiva. Background: churning clouds.
   - Part two: the sacrificial ground, scattered.
   - Part three: a vast, dim landscape of rivers and hills seen from above.
   - Part four: a mountain cave in snow.
3. **Palette:** `grief`, with ember accents in the Virabhadra section.
4. **Lighting:**
   - Lightning-like rim light on Shiva.
   - Virabhadra is seen as a towering column of light and shadow, not a monster.
   - The landscape is lit by moonlight, with points of light appearing.
   - Dramatic but never frightening.
5. **Camera:**
   - A close-up of Shiva's face; his eyes open (6 s).
   - A low-angle heroic rise as Virabhadra forms (8 s).
   - A fast wide shot as the sacrificial ground scatters (6 s).
   - A slow aerial drift over the land (20 s).
   - A slow push-in to Shiva meditating (10 s).
6. **Characters:**
   - Narada: a small, respectful figure with a veena.
   - Shiva's grief: shoulders bowed, then he rises. The hair-throw is one sweeping arc.
   - Wandering: Shiva walks slowly, carrying Sati's body in his arms.
     - Sati is drawn whole and at peace, as if asleep: eyes closed, face calm, wrapped in her red sari, her head resting against his shoulder.
     - There is no injury, no burning and no fire anywhere near her. Her sari trails a soft light.
     - Shiva's head is bowed over her. His steps are slow and heavy, about one every 2 s.
   - Vishnu: a calm, blue-skinned figure of soft light high in the sky, with one hand raised gently.
   - At the end Shiva sits, completely still.
7. **VFX:**
   - Embers and dissolving shadows as the altar scatters (pillars topple in silhouette; no gore).
   - Vishnu separating the body is shown gently, never as cutting. Each time his raised hand glows, a little of Sati's form dissolves into petals of light, starting from her sari's edge. The petals drift down to the land below. Her face stays calm until the last petals leave, and Shiva's arms are left holding only light.
   - Where each petal lands, a Shakti Peetha blooms as a small lotus of light, about 1 s apart. Two are highlighted and labelled Kamakhya and Kalighat.
   - Snow returns at the end.
   - Slow-motion window on the hair-throw (4 s).
8. **Transition:** the cave snow freezes into the still frame for Beat 7's choice.
9. **Intensity: 9** (Virabhadra), falling to 3 by the meditation.

**Music:**
- **Raga:** Marwa.
- **Instruments:**
  - Grief: sarangi-like strings, slow.
  - Virabhadra: climax layer at 96 BPM, with mridangam, low cinematic drums, rising strings and a single conch-like swell.
  - Wandering: the percussion exits and the tempo drops to 60; a solo sarangi line; a soft bell as each Peetha appears.
  - Meditation: the tanpura alone.
- **Dynamics:** mp, then f (never fortissimo), then p, then pp.
- **Register:** sorrowful, then heroic and fierce, then sorrowful, then meditative.
- **Cues:** a drum hit on the hair landing; bells on the Peetha lights.
- **Ambience:** storm wind, then quiet night.

## Beat 7: What will Shiva do with his grief? (choice, hypothetical)

1. **Script:** Beat 7: four options; "all four, in different texts"; the gods plead, the sacrifice is restored and Daksha repents (BP 4.7).
2. **Environment:** the snowy cave mouth at night. Four small lamps stand in a row in the foreground.
3. **Palette:** `grief`, warming slightly at the reveal.
4. **Lighting:** dim moonlight while the options are open. At the reveal, all four lamps light.
5. **Camera:** still while the options are open. At the reveal, a slow push past the lamps to an inset showing the restored altar fire (10 s).
6. **Characters:**
   - Inset: the gods as gentle silhouettes, palms joined.
   - Shiva, standing calm, with one hand raised in blessing over Daksha.
   - Daksha, restored, kneels before the fire with a goat's head in place of his own, as in temple iconography.
     - The goat's head is dignified and realistically proportioned, with gentle eyes and curved horns. The join at his neck is hidden by his garland and robes.
     - His palms are joined and his head bows slowly towards Shiva. A single tear of light falls as he asks forgiveness.

## Values to store per beat in `day1.ts`

- `intensity` (1–10)
- `palette` (a key from the table)
- `camera` (for example `pushIn`, `aerial`, `lowAngle`, `orbit` or `still`)
- `music` as `{ palette: 'indian' | 'hogwarts', raga?, bpm, layers: [...] }`

Freeze-frame times and the slow-motion window are given in seconds.

## Levels

- Every voice and layer has its own gain cap.
- The master bus runs through a compressor, then a limiter with its ceiling at −1 dBFS.
- The climax in Beat 6 is set to f, not ff.
- Peaks are checked in the build step (step 6).
