# Fact-check: Night 3 (docs/nights/night-3.md)

## 1. Summary

I checked about 75 factual claims in the lesson, the read-aloud script and the sources table. These covered the Shiva Purana wedding chapters (Rudra Samhita, Parvati Khanda 43–45, read in J. L. Shastri's translation on wisdomlib), the Devi Kavacham and Devi Mahatmya verse numbers, Chandraghanta's iconography and the popular wedding telling, the Neville scene (book and film transcript), Molly's duel, and the Dudley scene. The traditional material is accurate, and every verse number given checks out. **No High problems.** **1 Medium:** the doc says three times that the text calls Shiva's frightening form his "real form", but in the very next chapter Narada tells Mena it is *not* his real form. **6 Low:** small simplifications of order or wording, the bell in her hand (not in every iconographic list), "pushes the girls", and one unverified film detail. The one *to check* item, Neville's earlier lost points in the film, is resolved: the film leaves them out.

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

- **Beat 10 Film note, "the points Neville himself lost earlier. Check the film's version of that before mentioning it."** The film leaves this out. In the film McGonagall takes "50 points… each" and gives detention to "all four of you": Harry, Ron, Hermione and Malfoy. Neville isn't there and never tries to warn them. In the book (ch. 15) Neville was caught trying to warn Harry and Hermione about Malfoy, and he, Harry and Hermione each lost 50 points (Ron was in the hospital wing). So the retelling's line "Neville lost points too, trying to warn them about Malfoy" is true of the book only. Evidence: film transcript, https://www.springfieldspringfield.co.uk/movie_script.php?movie=harry-potter-and-the-sorcerers-stone ; book summary, https://www.hp-lexicon.org/thing/house-points-1991-1992/. A related film detail: Dumbledore says it takes "a great deal more" bravery to stand up to your friends, where the book has "just as much". The retelling follows the book, which is fine.

## 4. Theme (not to be changed)

- "In the Devi Mahatmya the bell protects by its sound alone: the ringing that overwhelms demons reassures the gods." The first half is well supported. DM 11.27 asks that the bell "which fills the world with its sound and destroys the might (tejas) of the demons" protect us "as a mother protects her children", and DM 2.56 has the Devi stupefying demons with her bell's sound. "Reassures the gods" is a fair reading of a prayer for protection, but the text doesn't say it in so many words. A minor point only. https://hindupad.com/devi-mahatmyam-durga-saptasati-chapter-11-in-english/ ; https://hindupad.com/devi-mahatmyam-durga-saptasati-chapter-2-in-english/
- Note for consistency: the theme says the bell "protects by its sound alone", but DM 4.24 lists the bell alongside spear, sword and bowstring. This doesn't contradict the theme, which rests on 11.27.

## 5. Checked and correct

- Devi Kavacham v. 3: "tṛtīyaṃ candraghaṇṭeti". Chandraghanta is the third name. https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_devii/dkavach.html
- Chandra = moon, ghanta = bell. The bell-shaped half-moon on her forehead gives the name (Gita Press *Nava Durga* via Wikipedia; Drik Panchang). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandraghanta
- Golden complexion, ten hands, lion; tiger or tigress in some sources; Manipura chakra linked with the third day (Gita Press via Wikipedia). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandraghanta
- Trishul, gada, sword, bow, arrow, lotus, japa mala, kamandalu, and an abhaya mudra hand (Drik Panchang list).
- The wedding link to Chandraghanta is popular and varies, and the Shiva Purana doesn't name her. Wikipedia notes that modern devotional accounts differ. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandraghanta
- The chapter titles and contents of SP Rudra Samhita, Parvati Khanda 43 ("Description of Śiva's wonderful sport"), 44 ("Menā regains consciousness") and 45 ("Śiva's comely form…") match the doc's chapter assignments (Shastri, wisdomlib doc226107–226109).
- Ch. 43: Mena watches from the terrace with Narada; gods in rising splendour; Indra, moon, sun, Brihaspati each "No"; Shiva notices her pride; ganas with many or no eyes and heads, wind-like, horns and damarus; bull, ash, skull, crescent moon in matted hair, tiger hide, elephant hide; "This is Śiva"; "I have been deceived"; faints; maids revive her. The sources table's vv. 45–65 range is correct.
- Ch. 44: Mena rebukes Narada, the seven sages and Parvati (Vishnu and other gods turned down, v. 15); threatens to kill her (vv. 22, 38, 48–50, 65–66); beats her (vv. 61–62); sages separate them (v. 63); Parvati: "your noble intellect has become perverted", Shiva lord of all, wooed "mentally, verbally, and physically" (vv. 52–60); gods, sages, Himavat, Brahma, Siddhas and Vishnu persuade her; condition "if He assumes a lovely form" (v. 101).
- Ch. 45: Vishnu urges Narada, Narada pleads, Shiva "joyously" takes a divine form (vv. 1–3); Mena "stunned as though drawn in a picture" (v. 21), praises the penance, asks pardon for her harsh words, bows (vv. 22–24); jubilation.
- DM 4.24: protect with spear, sword, sound of the bell, twang of the bowstring. DM 4.26: gentle (saumya) and very terrible (ghora) forms. Spoken after Mahishasura's death (HinduPad numbering). https://hindupad.com/devi-mahatmyam-durga-saptasati-chapter-4-in-english/
