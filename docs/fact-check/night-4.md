# Fact-check: Night 4 (docs/nights/night-4.md)

## 1. Summary

I checked about 60 factual claims across the lesson, the read-aloud script and the sources table. They covered the Kushmanda devotional account, the iconography, the etymology, the Rig Veda, Chandogya Upanishad, Devi Mahatmya and Brahma Sutra citations, Agra and Varanasi, the *Philosopher's Stone* chapter numbers and titles, both retellings sentence by sentence, and the film notes. I found no High-severity problems. There is 1 Medium problem: the Devi Mahatmya verse numbers are non-standard. There are 4 Low problems: one wording exaggeration, one small invented detail in an activity, one unconfirmed devotional detail, and one recap point that is a popular telling. The one *to check* item (Harry drawing a cake in the dust in the film) is resolved as correct. The Harry Potter retellings match the book closely. Caveat: web search hit a spend limit partway through, so some book-text and scripture checks rest on my knowledge of the standard texts and translations. Those are marked as such below.

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

- **Film: Harry draws a cake in the dust before midnight (Beat 8 film note; Sources row "Low *(to check)*").** **Correct.** In the 2001 film, Harry lies on the floor of the hut, draws a birthday cake with eleven candles in the dust, and at midnight blows the "candles" out ("Make a wish, Harry"), just before Hagrid bangs on the door. One secondary source describes the scene. It matches my own knowledge of the film, and I could not open a transcript. Evidence: https://lesliehand.com/blog/view/1394/harry_potter_s_birthday_cake. Confidence: medium-high. The Sources confidence could go up from Low to Medium.

## 5. Checked and correct

- Kushmanda's account is devotional, not Puranic: darkness everywhere, her smile, the cosmic egg (brahmanda), fourth of the nine forms. Gita Press *Nava Durga*, as summarised at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushmanda.
- The Devi Kavacham gives only the name, fourth in the list ("chaturthakam Kūṣmāṇḍeti"). Wikipedia Kushmanda, citing the Devi Kavacham.
- *Sūryamaṇḍala-antarvāsinī* = "she who dwells within the solar sphere"; she is the power in the sun, not Surya. Wikipedia Kushmanda.
- Eight arms / Ashtabhuja; water pot, bow, arrow, lotus, nectar pot, discus, mace, rosary; lion, with some images showing a tiger. Wikipedia Kushmanda.
- The rosary is linked with the eight siddhis and nine nidhis. Wikipedia Kushmanda.
- Night 4 and the Anahata chakra is a yogic interpretation. Wikipedia Kushmanda (citing *Nava Durga*).
- *kūṣmāṇḍa* = pumpkin/gourd (ash gourd, *Benincasa hispida*); the name is linked to the offering. Wikipedia Kushmanda; standard Sanskrit usage (Monier-Williams).
- *ku* + *ūṣma* + *aṇḍa* is a popular devotional etymology, correctly labelled as not grammar.
- RV 10.129.3: darkness hidden by darkness. 10.129.4: desire, the first seed of mind (*manaso retaḥ*). 10.129.6–7: "who really knows… perhaps he knows not", and the overseer "in the highest heaven". Griffith / standard translations (from knowledge).
- RV 10.121.1: Hiranyagarbha, the golden embryo, arose in the beginning. Griffith (from knowledge).
- Chandogya Upanishad 3.19: "the sun is Brahman"; non-being became an egg, which split into a silver half (earth) and a gold half (sky); the sun was born from it. Müller, SBE 1 (from knowledge).
- Devi Mahatmya ch. 1: Brahma's hymn says the Devi creates, sustains and consumes the universe at the end (verse number fix above). sanskritdocuments.org.
- Brahma Sutra 2.1.33: creation as mere play (*lila*). Standard text.
- Om as the first or primordial sound is a widely held teaching, correctly labelled as stated generally.
- Agra's *petha* is made from ash gourd. Common knowledge / food references.
- Durga Kund, Varanasi: built in the 18th century by Rani Bhabani of Natore (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Kund_Mandir). It is locally identified with Kushmanda (Wikipedia Kushmanda).
- Chapter numbers and titles (UK): 3 "The Letters from No One", 4 "The Keeper of the Keys", 6 "The Journey from Platform Nine and Three-Quarters", 12 "The Mirror of Erised". https://www.hp-lexicon.org/2011/07/10/philosophers-stone-chapter-chapter
- Ch. 3: the hut on the rock, the storm, Harry on the floor counting down on Dudley's watch, last year's coat-hanger and Uncle Vernon's old socks, the BOOM at midnight. HP Lexicon ps3; Wikibooks ch. 3.
- Ch. 4: door knocked off its hinges; slightly squashed box from Hagrid's coat; sticky chocolate cake, "Happy Birthday Harry" in green icing; "mighta sat on it"; Harry "meant to say thank you, but the words got lost" and asks who he is; fire, sausages, then "yer a wizard". Book text (from knowledge).
- Hagrid was sent by Dumbledore to deliver the letter; the book never says the cake was asked for. Book ch. 4.
- Film (2001): pink cake with misspelled green lettering ("Happee Birthdae Harry"); Hagrid jokes he may have sat on it and adds he baked it himself, "words and all". Film (from knowledge); search summaries.
- Ch. 6: Mrs Weasley first meets Harry briefly at King's Cross and explains the barrier. Book.
- Ch. 12: Harry expects no presents; a pile at the foot of the bed; the Dursleys send a 50p piece; a lumpy parcel holds a thick hand-knitted emerald-green sweater and homemade fudge; Ron: "I told her you didn't expect any presents"; maroon every year; Fred: Harry's is better, "more of an effort if you're not family". Wikibooks ch. 12; Goodreads quotation of Fred's line.
- Film: Ron says his mum made his jumper and Harry seems to have one too; the explanation and the fudge are book-only. Film (from knowledge).
- Harry gets a Weasley jumper in later years. Books 2–6 (from knowledge).
