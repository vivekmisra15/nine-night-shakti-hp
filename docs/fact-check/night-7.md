# Fact-check: Night 7 (docs/nights/night-7.md)

## 1. Summary

I checked about 75 factual claims across the lesson, the read-aloud script and the sources table. They cover the Devi Mahatmya chapters 1 and 5–8 (verse by verse against the Sanskrit and English on devimahatmyam.aurobharati.in), the Devi Kavacham, Kalaratri's iconography and epithets, and the Harry Potter references (PoA ch. 7, 8 and 17–19, plus ch. 22; DH ch. 32–33; PS ch. 8; GoF ch. 18; OotP ch. 28; film notes). The traditional retelling is very accurate. Every chapter and verse range I could check matches the text, including the less obvious details: Chandika laughing (8.53), the single "Katyayani" (8.29), the mace that does not hurt (8.58) and the "sacrifice of battle" (7.24). I found **0 High, 0 Medium and 8 Low** problems. They are mostly small imprecisions in the Harry Potter retellings, plus one verse number that varies by edition. An API spending limit cut the research short, so a few film details below are marked as checked only from the reviewer's knowledge.

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

The doc has no explicit *to check* items. Its Sources-table confidence notes were resolved as follows:

- **Ch. 1, v. 75 (hindupad numbering):** the numbering varies; v. 78 in the aurobharati edition. See the table above.
- **Film, Harry's boggart becomes a dementor before Lupin steps in (Medium-high):** consistent with the 2004 film as I know it. I was not able to fetch a film source before the limit.
- **Film, the talk moves out of the office and mentions Lily (Medium):** in the 2004 film, Lupin and Harry talk on the covered wooden bridge, and Lupin speaks about Lily. This is from the reviewer's knowledge and was not fetched, so treat it as **Unverified**, though likely correct.
- **Her two weapons (Medium; descriptions vary):** Drik Panchang gives a sword and an iron hook in the left hands; Wikipedia gives a scimitar and a thunderbolt. The doc's "a curved sword, and an iron hook or thunderbolt; descriptions vary" is accurate.

## 4. Theme (not to be changed)

I found no factual problem. "Kali didn't strike harder. She caught each drop" matches 8.53–61: Chandika strikes and Kali drinks the blood. "Unbound hair, three blazing eyes, fire on her breath" and "Shubhankari" match the devotional descriptions.

## 5. Checked and correct

Source for the Devi Mahatmya items: devimahatmyam.aurobharati.in, ch. 5–8.

- Shumbha and Nishumbha take the three worlds (5.2); Chanda and Munda see Ambika in the Himalaya (5.88–90); the marriage proposal by messenger (5.106–114); the vow "whoever conquers me in battle" (5.120).
- Dhumralochana is reduced to ashes by a *hum* sound (6.13); Shumbha orders Chanda and Munda to drag her by the hair or bind her (6.22–24). The doc's "vv. 13, 22–24" is exact.
- 7.3: she sits on the lion on a golden peak, smiling; 7.4: bows and swords drawn to seize her; 7.5: her face turns dark as ink.
- 7.6–8: Kali springs from the furrowed brow with sword and noose; gaunt, tiger skin, garland of skulls, wide mouth, sunken red eyes, a roar filling the directions. (The text also gives her a skull-topped staff; leaving it out is fine.)
- 7.10–13: she swallows elephants with their riders and chariots with their horses, and catches weapons in her mouth. 7.16–21: Chanda and Munda attack and are struck down.
- 7.23–24: the heads are offered in the "sacrifice of battle"; 7.26: the Devi speaks kindly; 7.27: the name Chamunda. The chapter has 27 verses.
- 8.4–6: Shumbha sends out all his clans of armies. 8.15–21: the Matrikas and their mounts (Brahmani swan, Maheshvari bull, Kaumari peacock, Vaishnavi Garuda, Varahi boar, Aindri elephant), matching the doc.
- "Katyayani" appears once in ch. 5–8 (8.29), so the doc's "once" is correct.
- 8.40–41: Raktabija steps forward, and each drop of his blood that falls produces a demon his size. 8.42: Aindri's thunderbolt; 8.47–48: Vaishnavi's discus and thousands of new demons; 8.52: the world is filled and the gods are afraid.
- 8.53: Chandika laughs and tells Chamunda to open her mouth wide. The Sanskrit reads *prāhasat tvarā*; some editions read *prāha satvarā* ("said quickly"), but the aurobharati text and translation support "laughed".
- 8.58: Raktabija's mace causes her no pain. 8.61–62: Kali drinks the blood and he falls, bloodless. 8.63: the gods rejoice. The chapter has 63 verses, and ch. 9–10 tell the battles with Nishumbha and Shumbha.
- Ch. 1: Brahma's hymn to Yoganidra (1.72–87) praises her as kalaratri, maharatri and moharatri, and asks her to release Vishnu to kill Madhu and Kaitabha (1.86–87). https://devimahatmyam.aurobharati.in/devimahatmyam/episode-1/chapter-1/
- The Devi Kavacham lists Kalaratri as the seventh of the nine forms (Kavacham v. 4, "saptamam kalaratriti"). https://hindupad.com/devi-kavacham/
- *Kala* means time and black; *ratri* means night. Kalaratri is dark, with loose hair, three eyes and flames from her breath, rides a donkey, and has abhaya and varada hands; she is called Shubhankari and makes devotees fearless. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaratri ; https://brhat.in/dhiti/kalaratri ; Drik Panchang (Kalaratri page)
- Linking Kalaratri to Raktabija in popular tellings is correctly labelled (brhat.in tells it that way; the Devi Mahatmya names Kali and Chamunda).
- PoA ch. 7 "The Boggart in the Wardrobe": laughter finishes a boggart; Riddikulus; Neville's Snape in his grandmother's clothes; Lupin steps in before it shapes for Harry; Lupin's boggart is the silvery orb or moon; Snape's staff-room jibe at Neville. (Wikibooks, ch. 7)
- PoA ch. 8 "Flight of the Fat Lady": tea in Lupin's office; Lupin expected Voldemort; Harry thought of the dementors; Harry fears fear itself. (Wikibooks, ch. 8)
- Lupin is revealed as a werewolf in PoA ch. 17–18. Snape lets it slip in ch. 22, "Owl Post Again".
- PoA ch. 17–19 titles are correct; Ron is dragged under the Whomping Willow to the Shrieking Shack; Pettigrew is Scabbers; Harry stops the killing ("my dad wouldn't have wanted his best friends to become killers", paraphrased) and Pettigrew is to go to the dementors.
- PS ch. 8 "The Potions Master"; GoF ch. 18 "The Weighing of the Wands" (Hermione's teeth, Snape's "I see no difference"); OotP ch. 28 "Snape's Worst Memory".
- DH ch. 32 "The Elder Wand": Nagini kills Snape in the Shrieking Shack and Harry is present (the DH2 film moves this to a boathouse). DH ch. 33 "The Prince's Tale": the friendship and the "Mudblood" rupture; the prophecy and the plea to Dumbledore; "never tell"; "Harry must die"; the doe Patronus ("Always"). The order in the retelling is correct.
- DH2 (2011) adds Snape cradling Lily's body at Godric's Hollow, which is not in the book. The epilogue names Albus Severus, with Snape "probably the bravest man I ever knew".
- Film years: Prisoner of Azkaban 2004; Deathly Hallows Part 2 2011.
