# Fact-check: Night 5 (docs/nights/night-5.md)

## 1. Summary

I checked about 75 factual claims in the lesson, the read-aloud script and the sources table. The Shiva Purana claims were checked verse by verse against J. L. Shastri's translation on wisdomlib (Rudra Samhita, Parvati Khanda ch. 15; Kumara Khanda ch. 2–5, 7, 9, 10). I also checked the Valmiki Ramayana (Bala Kanda 37), the Mahabharata (Vana Parva, Ganguli sections 224–229), the Harry Potter chapter references, and the 2007 film's dialogue against a subtitle transcript. Nearly every Shiva Purana chapter and verse number is right. I found **7 problems: 1 High, 2 Medium, 4 Low.**

- **High:** the Grimmauld Place retelling gets the key exchange the wrong way round. It is Mrs Weasley who says Harry is "as good as" her son, and Sirius who says Harry has him. The same mistake appears in the Beat 10 film note.
- **Medium:** the Mahabharata (Vana Parva) does not put the child in "the reeds of the Ganga". Svaha throws the seed into a golden lake on the White Mountain. Also, the film note implies the argument happens at dinner. In the film, the "not James / not your son" exchange is overheard through Extendable Ears before dinner.
- **Low:** some verse ranges are slightly off; Beat 7 tells Brahma's words and Taraka's taunt in the reverse of the text's order; one Tamil claim is credited to the Kanda Puranam when it comes from wider Tamil Shaiva tradition; and the script calls Nandin "the bull".

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

- **The name Gangeya (sources table, "Later texts and popular usage", Medium).** It is attested and is older than "later texts". Monier-Williams gives *Gāṅgeya* as a name of Skanda, citing the Mahabharata (Calcutta ed. ix 2465 and xiii 4096, i.e. Shalya and Anushasana Parvas). Shastri's Shiva Purana translation (Kumara Khanda ch. 5, note) explains that he is called "āgneya, kārttikeya, gāṅgeya and śaraja". Apte and the Shabda-Sagara also list Kartikeya as a meaning. Suggested source cell: "Mahabharata (Shalya and Anushasana Parvas); Sanskrit dictionaries; Shastri's Shiva Purana note". Confidence: High. Evidence: https://www.wisdomlib.org/definition/gangeya ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226125.html. (The name is not used anywhere in the lesson text itself, only in the table.)
- **Beat 10 film note, "Check how much of Lupin's and Mr Weasley's part survives".** Lupin's "general picture from us rather than a garbled version" argument and Mr Weasley's backing are **not in the film**, according to the subtitle transcript. Neither is Ginny being sent to bed. The dinner briefing lines (Fudge "not in his right mind", "fear makes people do terrible things", Voldemort rebuilding his army and recruiting) are kept, but subtitles do not show who says each line, so I can't confirm the speakers. Evidence: https://www.springfieldspringfield.co.uk/movie_script.php?movie=harry-potter-and-the-order-of-the-phoenix

## 4. Theme (not to be changed)

There are no factual problems. The description's claims match the sources:
- Agni, the Ganga, the six Krittikas and Parvati all carry or nurse him (Shiva Purana Kumara Khanda ch. 2–5).
- Parvati holds him, then the gods take him to war. Strictly, in Kumara Khanda ch. 5 v. 59 it is **Shiva** who "entrusted his son to the gods". "When the gods need him, she sends him out" is a reasonable reading for an interpretation-labelled theme, but it is not stated in the text. The doc already says this elsewhere ("The Shiva Purana does not tell us what Parvati said or felt").
- The vel as a spear holding her power matches Tamil Shaiva tradition (see the Low item above).

## 5. Checked and correct

Shiva Purana (Shastri translation, wisdomlib):
- Taraka's two boons: no equal in strength, and death only when a son of Shiva leads an army against him. Parvati Khanda ch. 15, vv. 40–41, exact. (doc226079)
- Taraka is crowned king of the three worlds (v. 44), takes Indra's Airavata (v. 48), and puts demons in the gods' places (v. 55); the gods flee to Brahma (v. 56). (doc226079)
- Agni takes the form of a dove and swallows the seed (Kumara Khanda ch. 2, vv. 9–11). Parvati's fury and curses are at vv. 12–21, exact. (doc226122)
- Six of the seven sages' wives (not Arundhati); Himavat; the Ganga; the forest of Śara reeds; born on the sixth day of the bright half of Margashirsha (vv. 55–68). (doc226122)
- The Krittikas come to bathe, quarrel, the boy grows six faces and drinks from all six, and he is "dearer to them than their own lives". Ch. 3, vv. 29–36, exact. (doc226123)
- The witnesses (Dharma and others) reveal where he is; Shiva's host encircles the house; the Krittikas are terrified; Nandin, as commander, gives the "dry tree ... fire" simile. Ch. 4, vv. 36–54. (doc226124)
- Kartikeya's reply: the Krittikas nursed him, he is their foster son and part of them, and he is not separated from Parvati, "virtually my mother". Ch. 4, vv. 63–65. (doc226124)
- The Krittikas go with him to Kailasa (ch. 5, vv. 8–11). Shiva kisses his head (v. 28); Parvati nurses him (v. 29) and takes him on her lap (v. 32); he teases Vasuki on Shiva's neck and Shiva laughs (vv. 35–36); Shiva is too moved to speak (v. 37). (doc226125)
- The gifts: Vishnu the discus, Shiva the trident, Indra an elephant and thunderbolt, Agni a spear "lovingly", Parvati power, prosperity and longevity, and the Krittikas gifts too. Ch. 5, vv. 40–52. (doc226125)
- The gods march with Kumara at their head. Ch. 7, vv. 2, 6, 8. (doc226127)
- Brahma: "neither a boy nor a youth but the lord of all"; born to kill Taraka. Ch. 9, vv. 4–5. (doc226129)
- Taraka sneers at the gods for putting "a child in front of me" and says the child should leave and save his life. Ch. 9, vv. 33–34. (doc226129)
- Kumara bows to Shiva and Parvati, takes up his spear, and kills Taraka; the universe is freed. Ch. 10, vv. 28–40. Shiva and Parvati each take him on their lap: vv. 42–43. (doc226130)
- No adult debates whether to send him. In ch. 5 v. 59, Shiva entrusts his son to the gods. (doc226125)

Ramayana and Mahabharata:
- Valmiki Ramayana, Bala Kanda 37: Vishvamitra tells it to Rama; Agni and the Ganga; the reed forest at the foot of the Himalaya; Indra and the gods arrange for the Krittikas (the "Krittika stars") to nurse him; the name Kartikeya; six faces to drink from all six. https://www.valmikiramayan.net/utf8/baala/sarga37/balasans37.htm
- Mahabharata, Vana Parva: Svaha takes the forms of six of the sages' wives, but cannot take Arundhati's (sec. 224). The sages divorce the six because of forest gossip (sec. 225). The six ask Skanda to be their son and he says "do you accordingly become my mothers" (sec. 229). https://www.ibiblio.org/sripedia/ebooks/mb/m03/m03225.htm , https://www.ibiblio.org/sripedia/ebooks/mb/m03/m03229.htm

Tamil tradition and iconography:
- Kanda Puranam: sparks become six boys in Saravana lake, are raised by the Krittikas, and are joined into one by Parvati. A secondary summary, matching the doc's own "Medium". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six_Abodes_of_Murugan
- Thaipusam honours the vel; Skanda Sashti is a six-day festival in Aippasi (Oct–Nov), ending in the Soorasamharam re-enactment. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaipusam
- Skandamata: four arms, two holding lotuses, one in abhaya, the child on her lap; she rides a lion and is seated on a lotus (Padmasani). The dhyana verse "siṁhāsanagatā nityaṁ padmāśritakaradvayā" matches "seated on a lion, holding lotuses in both hands". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skandamata
- The Vishuddha chakra link is a common devotional and yogic reading and is labelled interpretation. Not checked further.
