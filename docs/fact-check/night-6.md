# Fact-check: Night 6 (docs/nights/night-6.md)

## 1. Summary

I checked about 85 factual claims in the lesson, the read-aloud script and the sources table. They cover Devi Mahatmya chapters 2, 3, 4, 10 and 11 verse by verse (against the Aurobharati Sanskrit-English edition), the Bhagavata Purana 10.22 Katyayani vow (vedabase), the Vamana and Kalika Purana name origins, the iconography, *Order of the Phoenix* chapters 15–18, *Philosopher's Stone* chapters 16–17, and the film notes. The retellings and nearly all citations hold up. I found **1 High, 2 Medium and 6 Low** problems. The High one is a one-word slip ("Her generals fell" should say "His generals"). The Medium ones are the unresolved Vamana Purana chapter and the unconfirmed film-naming note. The Low ones are small verse-number offsets in DM ch. 3, the rosary giver (Prajapati, not Brahma), "each needed exactly once" (Hermione solves two obstacles), and an ambiguous sentence in the script.

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

- **Vamana Purana chapter (Beat 7).** Partly resolved: around ch. 18–21 depending on the edition. Wikipedia's footnote URL points to ch. 18, and Aufrecht's catalogue (cited in a 19th-century account) gives chs. 19–21. I did not reach a primary text, so this is still Medium. See row 2.
- **Film: how the name is chosen (OotP 2007).** Unresolved. The seized list headed "Dumbledore's Army" is very likely right; whether the film shows a naming moment is unverified. See row 3.

## 4. Theme (not to be changed)

- "Each god then gave her his own weapon" sits slightly against Beat 4: Shiva and Vishnu draw new weapons out of their own (DM 2.20–21), and gifts such as jewels, a lion and a cup are not weapons. This is a small imprecision only; the owner may leave it.
- "In a popular telling (not in the Devi Mahatmya), Mahisha had asked that only a woman could kill him": correct that this is not in the DM. The boon is not only popular, though: it is in the Devi Bhagavata Purana (Book 5). Nothing here is wrong.
- "The Devi Mahatmya says she is eternal and simply appears when she is needed": correct (DM 1.64–66, *nityaiva sā … āvirbhavati*).

## 5. Checked and correct

- Hundred-year war; Mahisha leads the asuras and takes Indra's place (DM 2.2–3); takes over sun, moon, fire, wind etc. (2.6); gods wander earth like mortals (2.7). Aurobharati ch. 2.
- Gods go to Brahma, then with him to Shiva and Vishnu, and ask for a plan (2.4–8).
- Vishnu's and Shiva's anger; radiance from the faces of Vishnu, Brahma and Shiva, then from Indra and the others; a mountain of fire; it becomes a woman filling the three worlds (2.9–13).
- Face from Shiva, hair from Yama, arms from Vishnu (2.14); three eyes from Agni (2.17).
- Gifts: Shiva's trident drawn from his own, Vishnu's discus, Varuna's conch, Agni's spear (2.20–21); Vayu's bow and quivers, Indra's thunderbolt and Airavata's bell, Yama's staff, Varuna's noose (2.22–23); Brahma's water pot (2.24); Kala's sword and shield, the Ocean's undecaying garments and jewels (2.25–27); Vishvakarma's axe and armour (2.28); the Ocean's lotus (2.29); Himavat's lion and Kubera's cup of wine, *aśūnyam* = never empty, so "the cup that never empties" is supported (2.30); Shesha's jewelled serpent necklace (2.31).
- Laugh, roar, worlds shaken, seas and mountains, gods cry "Victory" (2.32–35); "Ah, what is this?" and the charge (2.37); she fills the three worlds, thousand arms (2.38–39).
- Breaths become hosts of attendants (2.53); her lion roams the ranks (2.52).
- Buffalo form, horns hurling mountains and tearing clouds (3.21–28); noose, then lion, man with sword, elephant, buffalo again (3.29–33); she drinks and laughs (3.34); "roar while I drink" taunt (3.38); foot on neck and trident (3.40); half emerges from the mouth (3.41); beheaded by sword (3.42); army destroyed, gods rejoice, Gandharvas sing, Apsaras dance (3.43–44).
- Compassion in the heart, harshness in battle (DM 4.22); the boon to come in calamity, then she vanishes (4.35–39). Aurobharati ch. 4.
- Shumbha mocks her borrowed strength; "I am alone… these are my own powers"; the goddesses merge into her (DM 10.3–6). Aurobharati ch. 10.
- Gods praise Katyayani by name (DM 11.2); "this gentle (*saumya*) face adorned with three eyes… Katyayani" (11.25). Aurobharati ch. 11.
- Bhagavata Purana 10.22.1–6: unmarried gopis of Vraja, first month of winter (Margashirsha), the whole month, bathe in the Yamuna at sunrise, earthen image on the bank, pray for Nanda's son as husband. https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/10/22/
- Vamana Purana: gods' energies gather at Katyayana's hermitage, hence "daughter of Katyayana" (secondary sources). Wikipedia, Katyayani.
- Worship on the sixth night of Navaratri; four arms, lion, sword and lotus, abhaya and varada; Ajna chakra association; identified with Mahishasuramardini. Wikipedia, Katyayani.
- OotP ch. 15 "The Hogwarts High Inquisitor": Hermione proposes that Harry teach Defence; Harry protests it was luck and help. Wikibooks Muggles' Guide OotP ch. 15.
- OotP ch. 16 "In the Hog's Head": about 25 students from Gryffindor, Ravenclaw and Hufflepuff; Zacharias Smith sceptical; others list Harry's deeds; everyone signs Hermione's secrecy parchment. Wikibooks ch. 16.
- OotP ch. 17 "Educational Decree Number Twenty-Four": ban on unapproved student groups. Chapter title confirmed.
- OotP ch. 18 "Dumbledore's Army": Dobby tells Harry about the Room of Requirement; Harry elected leader; Cho's "Defence Association", Ginny's "Dumbledore's Army", playing on Fudge's fear; they begin with Expelliarmus. Wikibooks ch. 18.
- Umbridge's textbook is theory only (Slinkhard, *Defensive Magical Theory*); the Prophet calls Harry a liar; Harry is 15 in his fifth year.
- OotP film (2007): no Dobby, and Neville finds the Room of Requirement; Harry's "real life isn't like school" speech is moved to the Hog's Head (from memory of the film, consistent with fan summaries; https://geeksandgamers.com/harry-potter-and-the-order-of-the-phoenix-changes-from-book-to-film/).
- PS ch. 16 "Through the Trapdoor": order of obstacles; Hermione's fire against the Devil's Snare; Harry and the flying key; Ron sacrifices himself at chess; the troll already knocked out; Hermione's potion riddle with enough for one person; her line about books and cleverness versus friendship and bravery; she goes back for Ron.
