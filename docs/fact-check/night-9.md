# Fact-check: Night 9 (docs/nights/night-9.md)

## 1. Summary

I checked about 70 factual claims in the lesson, the read-aloud script, the sources table and the series look-back. They cover Siddhidatri's iconography; Bhagavata Purana 11.15.3–5 and 11.15.33; Yoga Sutra 3.37/3.38; the Ardhanarishvara story in the Shiva Purana (Shatarudra Samhita ch. 3); Devi Mahatmya ch. 1 and 13, verse by verse; Vijayadashami and its regional customs; Ayudha Puja; *Deathly Hallows* ch. 35–36 and the *Deathly Hallows Part 2* film; and *Philosopher's Stone* ch. 12 and 17 and the 2001 film. The core texts and the Harry Potter retellings hold up well. Every Devi Mahatmya ch. 13 verse number (13.9–12, 13.17, 13.18, 13.20–25) matches. I found **0 High, 2 Medium and 6 Low** problems. The Medium problems are (a) giving the Devi Mahatmya as the source for "the stories of Katyayani, Kalaratri" (the text uses those names only as epithets in its hymns) and (b) an unverified "David Heyman on the change" citation. I resolved two of the three *to check* items (the film does not mend the wand, and the film's King's Cross cuts Dumbledore's confessions). I could not confirm the Devi Purana attribution.

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

1. **"The film does not show him mending his old wand."** Confirmed. In *Deathly Hallows Part 2* Harry snaps the Elder Wand and throws it off the bridge, and does not repair his own wand first. The film also skips the visit to Dumbledore's portrait. Evidence: https://geekdad.com/2011/07/comparing-harry-potter-and-the-deathly-hallows-part-2-the-book-and-the-movie/ ; also https://warnerbros.fandom.com/wiki/Elder_Wand (snaps it in two and throws it over the bridge).
2. **"Dumbledore's words about power come from the book (to check that the film leaves them out)."** Largely confirmed. The film's limbo scene "explains less", and Dumbledore's confessions about Grindelwald and his hunt for the Hallows were cut (GeekDad, above). To my knowledge the film also omits the Minister-for-Magic refusal and the "those best suited to power" line. I could not reach a transcript (fandom and transcript sites blocked), so treat the last point as **high-probability but not quoted from a source**.
3. **"Sometimes attributed to the Devi Purana."** **Unverified.** No textual source found. See the Low row above, which suggests dropping the attribution.

## 4. Theme (not to be changed)

No factual problems.
- "the Devi Mahatmya calls the Goddess 'the cause of liberation'": correct. DM 1.44 (1.57 in other numberings): *sā vidyā paramā mukter hetubhūtā sanātanī*, "she is the supreme knowledge, the eternal cause of liberation". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya
- "The Yoga Sutras warn they can become obstacles": correct (YS 3.37/3.38).
- "In the Devi Gita, the Goddess teaches that the self in every being is one with the infinite, and that knowing this sets you free": a fair summary of the Devi Gita (Devi Bhagavata Purana 7.31–40), which teaches that self and Devi/Brahman are one and that knowledge liberates. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Gita
- "In a devotional account, even Shiva received his powers through her": correctly framed as devotional.
- The wave/ocean image is a common Vedantic simile. "An old image" is fair.

## 5. Checked and correct

- Siddhidatri: four arms with discus, conch, mace and lotus; seated on a lotus or riding a lion; flanked by gods, gandharvas, yakshas, siddhas and asuras; worshipped on the ninth day. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhidhatri
- *siddhi* = perfection/accomplishment, *datri* = giver: standard etymology.
- BP 11.15.3: eighteen siddhis, eight primary. BP 11.15.4–5: the eight (anima, mahima, laghima, prapti, prakamya, ishita, vashita, kamavasayita), so the BP has no *garima* and its eighth differs from popular lists, as the sources table says. https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/15/
- BP 11.15.33: experts call these perfections obstacles and a waste of time for the highest yoga, spoken by Krishna to Uddhava. https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/11/15/
- Yoga Sutra 3.37 (3.38 in some numberings): *te samādhāv upasargā vyutthāne siddhayaḥ*, powers in the outward state and obstacles to samadhi. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga_Sutras_of_Patanjali
- Shiva Purana, Shatarudra Samhita ch. 3: Brahma's creation will not multiply; he does tapas on Shiva united with Shakti; Shiva appears as Ardhanarishvara; Brahma asks the Goddess for the power to create the female race and to be born as Daksha's daughter; she grants it and merges back into Shiva's body. Section labels differ between editions, as the doc notes. https://www.greenmesg.org/bharatavarsha/scriptures/puranas/shiva_purana/shata_rudra_samhita/ardhanareeshwara_incarnation.php
- Daksha is Brahma's son, and Daksha's daughter is Sati.
- DM ch. 1: Suratha loses his kingdom and his ministers turn against him; he rides alone into the forest; meets Samadhi, a merchant cast out by his greedy wife and sons; both still worry about those who wronged them; they ask the sage Medhas. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Mahatmya
- DM 13.9–12: sandbank of a river, earthen image, flowers, incense, fire and water, fasting or eating little, minds fixed, three years. DM 13.13: Chandika appears. 13.17: Suratha's request. 13.18: knowledge removing "mine" and "I". 13.20–25: the boons, including "after a few days" the kingdom and rebirth from the Sun as Savarni Manu. 13.28: she vanishes. 13.29: closing. https://www.astroyogi.com/spirituality/durga-saptashati/chapter-13
- The DM ends with ch. 13 (13 chapters, 700 verses).
- Vijayadashami = *vijaya* + *dashami*, Shukla Dashami of Ashvin; marks the Devi's victory over Mahishasura (DM ch. 2–3); popularly also Rama's victory and Arjuna's retrieval of the weapons from the shami tree. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami
- North India: Ramlila ends; effigies of Ravana, Kumbhakarna (brother) and Meghnad (son) are burned. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami
- Bengal: Bijoya Dashami ends Durga Puja; Durga's autumn visit to her parental home with her children and return to Shiva; immersion; sindoor khela; Bijoya greetings. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Puja
- Kerala: Vidyarambham, with first letters written in rice or sand. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidyarambham
- Karnataka: Mysuru Dasara procession (Jamboo Savari) and illuminated palace; *gombe* doll displays. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Dasara
- Maharashtra: apta (*Bauhinia racemosa*) leaves exchanged as "gold". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayadashami
- Himachal: Kullu Dussehra begins on Vijayadashami, with processions of village deities. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kullu_Dussehra
- Ayudha Puja on Mahanavami (ninth day) in much of South India; tools, books and vehicles honoured. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayudha_Puja
- *Deathly Hallows* ch. 35 "King's Cross" and ch. 36 "The Flaw in the Plan" (UK titles).
- Fred, Lupin and Tonks have died before Harry walks into the forest (ch. 31–34).
- Ch. 35: bright, misty place that Harry says looks like King's Cross; Dumbledore's confession of his youthful hunger for power and its mistakes; his repeated refusal of the post of Minister for Magic because he could not trust himself with power; "those best suited to power" have never sought it, like Harry.
- Ch. 36: Harry became master of the Elder Wand by overpowering and disarming Draco at Malfoy Manor; in the headmaster's office he repairs his holly-and-phoenix wand with the Elder Wand; he plans to return it to Dumbledore's tomb so its power ends if he dies a natural death undefeated; Ron is incredulous ("mad"); Harry says the wand is more trouble than it's worth; he left the Resurrection Stone in the forest; he urged Voldemort to try for remorse.
- Film *Deathly Hallows Part 2* (2011): Harry snaps the Elder Wand on the bridge with Ron and Hermione and throws the pieces away. https://geekdad.com/2011/07/comparing-harry-potter-and-the-deathly-hallows-part-2-the-book-and-the-movie/
- Harry wanted to be an Auror (*Order of the Phoenix*, careers advice); he chose his house by asking the Sorting Hat not to put him in Slytherin (PS ch. 7).
- *Philosopher's Stone* ch. 12 "The Mirror of Erised" and ch. 17 "The Man with Two Faces" (UK titles); film 2001.
- Ch. 12: Harry sees his family behind him in the Mirror at Christmas; Dumbledore explains it shows the heart's deepest desire, that people have wasted away before it, and asks him not to look for it again.
- Ch. 17: beyond the trapdoor, Quirrell stands at the Mirror; Voldemort taught him there is no good and evil, only power; he sees himself presenting the Stone to his master but can't get it; Harry's reflection pockets the Stone and winks, and the real Stone drops into Harry's pocket; in the hospital wing Dumbledore explains that only someone who wanted to find the Stone, not use it, could get it; the Stone is destroyed with Flamel's agreement, and Nicolas and Perenelle will die once their Elixir runs out. Harry is eleven.
- The 2001 film keeps the Christmas Mirror scenes, Quirrell's "only power" line, the pocket moment and Dumbledore's hospital-wing explanation of the Mirror's rule (recalled from the film; consistent with the doc).
