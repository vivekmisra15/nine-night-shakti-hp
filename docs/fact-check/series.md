# Fact-check: series-wide text

Files: `docs/NINE_NIGHTS_CONTENT.md`, `docs/PARENT_PREP.md`, `docs/DISCLAIMER.md`, `src/content/journey.ts`. Checked 18 October 2026. No project file was edited.

## 1. Summary

I checked about 150 factual claims across the four files. They cover every Harry Potter chapter number and title in the plan and the prep sheet, the film titles and years, the film notes, the scene details in the night-by-night summaries, the Puranic citations (Bhagavata Purana 4.3–4.7, the Shiva Purana Rudra Samhita chapters, Devi Mahatmya chs. 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, Skanda Purana Prabhasa Khanda ch. 68, the Kalika Purana via Hazra), the nine Devanagari spellings and name meanings, festival practice, the nine-colours origin, the rights holders in the disclaimer, and the spoiler and opening text. I found **no High problems, 1 Medium and 9 Low**. The Medium one is in a "Still to check" note marked as resolved: it says Bhagavata Purana 4.7.58 names the Himalayas "but not Himavat", yet the Sanskrit word there is *himavataḥ*, "of Himavat". The Low problems are small retelling slips and wording mismatches between the docs. I also resolved two open *to check* items: the Devi Kavacham's Varaha Purana attribution is confirmed, and the DH Part 2 film does not show Harry mending his wand. A third, the Matsya and Shiva Purana teasing versions, is only partly resolved.

## 3. Resolved *to check* items

- **Devi Kavacham attributed to the Varaha Purana:** confirmed. The standard text, transcribed from the Gita Press Saptashati (and *Mantra Maharnava*, Khemraj 1924), ends "iti śrīvārāhapurāṇe hariharabrahmaviracitaṃ devyāḥ kavacaṃ sampūrṇam". Verses 3–5 list the nine names in the app's order. The hymn is not found in the extant Varaha Purana, so "traditionally attributed" is the right wording. Evidence: https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_devii/chaNDiikavacham.itx (header: "vArAhapurANe, Gitapress saptashati").
- **Does the DH Part 2 film show Harry mending his wand?** No. In the film Harry snaps the Elder Wand and throws it off the bridge without repairing his holly wand. Evidence: https://movieweb.com/harry-potter-books-films-differences/ ; https://www.dallasobserver.com/arts-culture/book-versus-film-eight-major-misfires-in-harry-potter-and-the-deathly-hallows-part-2-7086513/ . The CONTENT Night 9 "Film difference" line can drop "(whether the film shows the mended wand is still to check)", and the prep sheet's note is already correct.
- **Bhagavata Purana 4.7 rebirth:** the reference is right (4.7.58), but the note "not Himavat" is wrong; see Problems.
- **Shiva Purana and Matsya Purana versions of Shiva teasing Parvati:** partly resolved. The Matsya Purana chapter list has ch. 155 "Quarrel between Siva and Parvati" and ch. 157 "Parvati's curse on Viraka and boon to Ekanamsa", where the Kali/Gauri episode sits (https://vedamsbooks.com/books/matsya_mahapurana_an_exhaustive_introduction_sanskrit_text_english_translation_scholarly_notes_and_index_of_verses). For the Shiva Purana, I believe the episode is in the Vayaviya Samhita, but I couldn't confirm the chapter: **Unverified**. The Kalika Purana ch. 47 (Hazra) is confirmed, with one nuance: there Urvashi and the Apsarases mock Kali's dark colour, and Shiva bathes her in the celestial Ganga to give her a golden complexion. Shiva does not do the teasing in that version. CONTENT's wording ("In the Kalika Purana, Shiva bathes her in the heavenly Ganga and gives her a golden glow") is accurate.
- **Shiva Purana, Shiva's beautiful form (Night 3):** confirmed. Rudra Samhita, Parvati Khanda ch. 45: Vishnu urges Narada, Narada pleads, and Shiva takes a comely form. Mena faints in ch. 43, v. 64. https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226109.html ; https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226107.html
- **Night 2 tests and Aparna:** confirmed. Ch. 22 v. 49: she gave up leaves, "so she was called Aparna" (https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226086.html). Ch. 25 is "The seven celestial sages test Parvati". In ch. 26 Shiva comes as a Jatila, a very old brahmin, and Parvati also addresses him as a *brahmacarin* (https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/shiva-purana-english/d/doc226090.html). "Disguised as an old ascetic" is correct; the "Kalidasa makes him a young brahmacharin" contrast is a little overstated, since the Shiva Purana also uses the word.

## 4. Theme (not to be changed)

These are factual points only, for the owner's information. None needs the theme changed.

- **Night 3 (Readiness):** "weapons in every hand" is slightly imprecise. In the standard Chandraghanta iconography some of her ten hands hold a lotus, a rosary or a water pot, and one shows *abhaya*. The bell verse that fits "protects by its sound alone... overwhelms demons" best is DM 11.27 (*hinasti daityatejāṃsi svanenāpūrya yā jagat*). Ch. 4 v. 24 (cited in the night summary) is the gods asking for protection "by the sound of your bell". Both are in the Devi Mahatmya, so the theme stands.
- **Night 6 (Courage):** "each of them, alone, had lost". DM 2.2–3 says gods and asuras fought for a full hundred years, with Indra leading the gods, so the gods lost together, not one by one. "In a popular telling (not in the Devi Mahatmya), Mahisha had asked that only a woman could kill him" is right that it isn't in the Devi Mahatmya. But it is also a Purana account (Devi Bhagavata Purana, book 5), not only a popular telling. https://hindupad.com/devi-mahatmyam-durga-saptasati-chapter-2-in-sanskrit/ ; https://kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/devibhagvatpurana37.html
- **All other themes:** no factual problem found. The Devi Mahatmya "cause of liberation" is 1.56 (hindupad numbering). "Eternal and simply appears when needed" is 1.61–63. Yoga Sutra 3.37/3.38 calls the siddhis obstacles to samadhi.

**Tradition**
- Bhagavata Purana: Shiva warns Sati (4.3), she leaves her body by yogic fire (4.4), Virabhadra destroys the sacrifice (4.5), and she is reborn to Mena and Himavat (4.7.58). Daksha gave his daughter at Brahma's word (4.2.16) (vedabase.io).
- Shiva carrying Sati's body comes from later texts (Devi Bhagavata 7.30, Kalika Purana).
- Shiva Purana, Rudra Samhita: Sati, Parvati and Kumara Khandas; Aparna (Parvati Kh. 22.49); the sages' test (ch. 25); Shiva as Jatila (ch. 26); Mena faints (ch. 43); Narada persuades Shiva, urged by Vishnu (ch. 45) (wisdomlib).
- Kumarasambhava 1.26: the "u ma" origin of the name Uma.
- Devi Mahatmya:
  - ch. 1 v. 75 *kālarātrir mahārātrir...* (hindupad numbering; other editions number it differently)
  - ch. 2: Shiva's trident, Vishnu's discus, Indra's vajra and the bell from Airavata (v. 22), Himavat's lion (v. 29)
  - ch. 3: Mahisha slain
  - ch. 4 v. 24: the bell verse
  - ch. 5: Kaushiki emerges and Parvati becomes dark, as Kalika
  - ch. 7: Kali from the brow, Chanda and Munda
  - ch. 8: Raktabija
- Skanda Purana, Prabhasa Khanda ch. 68: Shiva jokingly calls her Kali, she is Sati of her earlier birth, and she becomes fair through tapas with no bath (wisdomlib). The Kalika Purana ch. 47 bath is confirmed via Hazra.
- Katyayani: the Vamana Purana hermitage of Katyayana, the Kalika Purana variant (Katyayana as first worshipper), and the gopis' vow in Bhagavata Purana 10.22 (Wikipedia, Katyayani).
- Skanda's birth: Agni, then Ganga, then the reed forest and the Krittikas, with six faces. Versions in the Ramayana Bala Kanda and the Mahabharata Vana/Aranyaka Parva (with Svaha). Thaipusam marks Parvati giving the vel, and Skanda Sashti marks the defeat of Surapadman.
- Kushmanda: *kūṣmāṇḍa* = ash gourd, the popular *ku-ushma-anda* etymology, eight arms, lion, the core of the sun. Hiranyagarbha is Rig Veda 10.121.
- Siddhidatri: the eight siddhis (anima, laghima...); discus, conch, mace and lotus; seated on a lotus or lion; the Ardhanarishvara link is devotional only (Wikipedia cites no Purana).
- Iconography of the other forms:
  - Shailaputri: bull, trident, lotus.
  - Brahmacharini: white, rosary, water pot.
  - Chandraghanta: crescent bell on her forehead, ten arms, tiger or lion.
  - Skandamata: child on her lap, lotuses, lion.
  - Kalaratri: dark, loose hair, three eyes, fiery breath, donkey, abhaya and varada hands, Shubhankari.
  - Mahagauri: white, bull, trident and damaru.
- Festival: Ayudha Puja on Mahanavami, Vidyarambham on Vijayadashami (Kerala), Bijoya Dashami immersion (Bengal), Ravana effigies (north), golu and garba across the nine nights, Kanya Puja.
- The nine colours began in 2003 as a *Maharashtra Times* campaign (The Quint).
- Devanagari: all nine spellings are correct (शैलपुत्री, ब्रह्मचारिणी, चन्द्रघण्टा, कूष्माण्डा, स्कन्दमाता, कात्यायनी, कालरात्रि, महागौरी, सिद्धिदात्री), and they match the Kavacham.
- Strength count: "five strong, three partial / partial to strong, one speculative" matches the table.
